How do you keep Reddit and X going every day without living on them?

I couldn't. So I built myself an agent team that does it. I send it ideas (often a voice note while walking), it turns them into draft posts with one diagram each, and nothing goes out until I approve it, or approve with a note it applies. Then it posts and sends me one digest in the evening.

It runs on EpicSpine, the way my agents keep goals and state in the repo instead of in my head.

I'm looking for a few testers to run it on their own accounts during the launch. It is early and rough, so I'd rather have five people who really use it than fifty who sign up. That is why this is an application, not a signup: tell me how you'd use it and I'll pick the ones it fits best.

What you get

  • Your own team (Scout, Writer, Publisher, Responder) for your Reddit and/or X, in your own private space that only you log into.
  • Your voice, your approval: every post is a draft until you say yes, no, or "yes, but shorter".
  • Set up with me: we write your brief together on a short call, and you talk to me directly while it is rough.

What I'd expect from you

  • Feed it: a few ideas a week (a sentence or a voice note is enough).
  • Approve or comment on drafts, ideally within a day, so posts go out on time.
  • Tell me what's broken: a few lines of feedback every week, or a 15-minute call.
  • Stay about four weeks: the launch window, November 2026.

What you need to be comfortable with

  • Agents writing in your name: they draft from your ideas and your brief; you approve every post before it goes out.
  • One login step: you log into your X and/or Reddit once in a private browser that only your team uses, so it can post what you approved. You can log it out any time. Until you do, you still get the drafts and can post them yourself.
  • Rough edges: things will break; I fix them fast and tell you.

Why $10/month

Running your team costs real compute: I expect to spend around $50 on each tester. The $10/month doesn't cover that and isn't meant to. It's there so testers are people who will actually use it, and so I learn whether this is worth paying for. Nothing to pay now: if you're picked, I send a payment link when your team is ready to run. Stop any time.

Real-life case study (optional)

During the launch I'd like to show one or two real teams at work: what went in, what went out, what worked. You read and OK every line before anything is published, and it's fine to say no; it doesn't change whether you're picked.
